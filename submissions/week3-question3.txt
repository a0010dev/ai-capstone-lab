I tested my industrial reporting agent on representative business questions
using synthetic manufacturing data (synthetic-defects-v1), not production ERP
data. The notebook contains eleven saved runs dated 18 October 2026 using
gpt-4o-mini. Three queries covering different tool paths were:

1. Main tool / summary
   Question: "Dona'm el resum de defectes de la secció TE de l'1 al 30 de setembre de 2026."
   Tool: get_defect_summary
   Outcome: 3 controls, sample quantity 220, 23 errors, 19 affected,
   4 repaired and 12 removed board occurrences.

2. Different tool / Pareto ranking
   Question: "Mostra el Pareto de defectes de TE de setembre de 2026, amb percentatges i acumulats."
   Tool: get_defect_pareto with metric="removed_boards"
   Outcome: DEMO-CODE-C=8, DEMO-CODE-A=3, DEMO-CODE-B=1.
   Their shares were 66.67%, 25.00% and 8.33%, with cumulative shares
   of 66.67%, 91.67% and 100.00%. These are contributions to recorded
   removals, not defect rates or evidence of causes.

3. Edge case / Pareto with a zero total
   Question: "Mostra el Pareto de retirades de TE de l'1 al 31 d'octubre de 2026."
   Tool: get_defect_pareto with metric="removed_boards"
   Outcome: the tool correctly returned a source record with zero removals
   and undefined percentages (null), but the model incorrectly described
   the result as having no matching records.

What I learned

The tool returned DEMO-DEF-006 under DEMO-CTRL-006 with quantity 0.
The model listed that same record, then contradicted itself by saying
"Sense registres coincidents; cobertura desconeguda."
A zero selected metric does not mean an empty selection. Its percentage
contributions are undefined because the denominator is zero. The tools'
results were correct; the error appeared in the model's interpretation.
Next week I want to distinguish zero totals from empty selections explicitly
in response validation and present coverage information deterministically.
